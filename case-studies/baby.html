The gap

Here's the thing about baby products: the customer's needs change every few months, whether the brand keeps up or not. A newborn needs one thing. A baby who's sitting up needs something else. A crawling, teething, almost-walking baby needs something else again. This brand sent the same emails to everyone, no matter how old their baby was — so by month four, half the list had stopped opening anything.

On top of that: a huge share of "customers" were never the parent at all. Grandparents, aunts, friends — buying off a gift registry for someone else's baby. They got a receipt. They never got a second email, because the brand had no idea they weren't the parent.

What we ran

  • Baby's age, captured once — a simple due-date or birth-month question at checkout, so the brand always knows what stage this baby is actually in
  • Stage flows, not one welcome series — a new email arrives right as the baby hits the next stage, showing the next thing they'll actually need, before the parent has to go looking for it
  • Two different people, two different flows — gift-givers get a warm thank-you and a "here's what else this family might love" — parents get the stage-by-stage journey
  • Registry follow-up — everyone who views a shared registry now gets a gentle nudge, not just a static wishlist page nobody follows up on

The result

Repeat purchase at the next baby stage nearly quadrupled. Registry views — which used to go nowhere — now drive 64% more revenue on their own. The list didn't get bigger. It just started getting emails that actually matched what was happening in each family's life.
